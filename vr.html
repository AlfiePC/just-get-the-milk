Just Get the Milk VR

Find three things in a supermarket and pay. Easy. Except you are shopping as an autistic young person, and the shop was not built for you. Each game deals you a different sensory profile.

WalkL stickPush the left thumbstick to walk, the right to turn. Or press B and you are walked to the next item.
Pick upTriggerWalk up to the bouncing item, look at it and pull the trigger.
CopeAXWhen the shop gets too much, tap A or X as fast as you can. One challenge each game cannot be won, whatever you do.
Loud, layered sound and pulsing light. Meant to be uncomfortable. Headphones on, volume low to start. Not for anyone with photosensitive epilepsy, motion sickness or their own sensory sensitivities. Someone should be with you. Taking the headset off stops everything.
Settings and the research behind this
No pulsing light, gentler sound, no camera shake

We do not know how any one autistic young person experiences the world. Some find sound painful, others barely notice it and go looking for more. So each game deals you a different sensory profile. Built on first-hand accounts and research including the University of Reading supermarket study (2022) and reviews of hyperacusis and sensory processing in autism. Sources are listed at the end.

      The person you were this time

      Six senses, each turned up or down. Play again and you get a different mix. Real young people are more complex than this, and it shifts with tiredness, illness or anxiety.

      What hit you, and why

      The task was never the hard part.

      Three items on a list. What made it hard was a place built for a sensory profile that is not everyone's. Autistic young people with complex needs live with this every day, and overload adds up, so one more small noise after a hard morning can tip into crisis. At Prior's Court we design the environment around each young person, not the other way round.
